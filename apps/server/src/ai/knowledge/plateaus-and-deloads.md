# Plateaus and Deloads

A plateau is a period where an exercise's working weight and reps stop moving upward across several consecutive sessions despite consistent training. A single flat session is normal variation, not a plateau — the pattern generally needs to hold for at least three to four sessions before it is worth treating as one.

Plateaus have a few common causes: accumulated fatigue that has not been addressed with a deload, insufficient recovery (sleep, food, stress), a training program that has stopped providing enough stimulus, or simply a wall that most lifters hit as they get closer to their natural strength ceiling for a given rep range.

Interventions differ depending on the likely cause. If fatigue signals are present alongside the plateau, a deload usually resolves it within one to two weeks. If fatigue is not evident, changing the rep range, exercise variation, or weekly volume for that muscle group for several weeks often breaks through a stimulus-based plateau. Simply repeating the same weight and hoping for a different outcome rarely works on its own.

Not every plateau needs an aggressive fix. For lifters with years of training experience, slower rates of progress on a given lift are expected and not necessarily a problem to solve — the more useful question becomes whether overall training consistency and total volume are still trending in the right direction.
