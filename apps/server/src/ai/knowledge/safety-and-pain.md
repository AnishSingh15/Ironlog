# Training Safety and Pain

There is an important difference between normal training discomfort and pain that signals a possible injury. Muscle soreness that peaks 24-48 hours after a session and fades within a few days, a mild burning sensation during high-rep sets, and general fatigue are all expected parts of training.

Sharp, sudden, or localized joint pain during a lift, pain that persists or worsens between sessions, swelling, numbness, tingling, or pain that changes the way someone walks or moves outside the gym are different in kind, not just degree. These are signals to stop the aggravating movement and consult a qualified medical professional — a physiotherapist or doctor — rather than something to train through or self-diagnose from workout history.

Workout data such as weights, reps, and session frequency can show patterns like a sudden drop in performance or an exercise being avoided, but that data cannot determine whether a symptom is a soft-tissue injury, a joint issue, or something else. Any tool working from training data alone should flag possible concerns and recommend professional evaluation rather than offering a diagnosis or medical treatment plan.

When in doubt, the safer default is to modify or skip a specific exercise and keep training other movements that do not aggravate the issue, rather than stopping all training or pushing through pain to preserve a streak.
