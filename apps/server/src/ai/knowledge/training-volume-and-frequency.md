# Training Volume and Frequency

Training volume — typically measured as sets per muscle group per week — is one of the strongest predictors of muscle growth, up to a point where additional volume stops helping and starts interfering with recovery.

For most lifters, somewhere between 10 and 20 hard sets per muscle group per week drives good progress, with beginners doing well near the lower end and more experienced lifters often needing the upper end to keep progressing. Volume should be built up gradually over months, not weeks — jumping straight to 20 sets per muscle group after a break in training is a common cause of excessive soreness and missed sessions, not faster gains.

Frequency — how often a muscle group is trained per week — matters mostly because it lets total volume be split into more manageable sessions. Training a muscle group twice a week with fewer sets per session is generally as effective as training it once a week with more sets per session, and often easier to recover from.

Consistency compounds: three sessions a week performed reliably for a year outperforms a "perfect" five-day split that only gets followed half the time. When a program's frequency or volume is not sustainable given a person's schedule, reducing the plan to fit the schedule beats abandoning it.
